## Summary

*   Purpose: Introduce the Vascular Modeling Toolkit (VMTK) and PyVista libraries for simulating blood flow and analyzing cardiovascular models.
*   Key Subjects: Using VMTK for centerline extraction, visualizing simulations with Paraview and PyVista, and selecting appropriate anatomical models.
*   Major Outcomes: Participants will learn to extract center lines from simulated blood flow models using VMTK and visualize the results using Paraview and PyVista.
*   Immediate Next Steps: Breakout groups will vote for a leader and begin brainstorming model choices and preparing questions.

## Discussion

### Vascular Modeling Toolkit & PyVista

*   The session focused on utilizing VMTK and PyVista for geometric analysis and visualization of cardiovascular simulations.
*   VMTK is crucial for extracting centerline data from CFD simulations, a key step in the modeling process.
*   PyVista is used for interactive 3D visualization of the models and simulation results, allowing for exploration and analysis.
*   Paraview is recommended for manual visualization of the 3D models.

### Anatomical Model Selection

*   Participants will select a single anatomical model to work on, focusing on simplicity initially.
*   Recommended models include aorta, coronary arteries, cerebral arteries, and pulmonary arteries.
*   The session highlighted the importance of selecting models with visible branches.
*   The example model presented was a six-year-old female with hypoplastic left heart syndrome, illustrating a real-world application of CFD.

### Group Structure & Leadership

*   Two breakout rooms were created, one for each group (2A and 2B).
*   Each group will elect a leader to facilitate discussions and decision-making.
*   Leaders are responsible for guiding the group, not for completing all the work.
*   Participants are encouraged to prepare questions in advance.

## Decisions

*   VMTK and PyVista are the primary tools for centerline extraction and visualization.
*   Paraview is recommended for manual visualization.
*   Each group will select a single anatomical model.
